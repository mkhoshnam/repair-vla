Abstract

Vision-language-action (VLA) policies are typically deployed under the assumption that the robot body realizes commanded actions similarly to the conditions seen during training. Persistent actuator faults violate this assumption: the policy may continue to predict semantically reasonable actions while a damaged or constrained actuator changes the action-to-motion map. We study whether task-reward reinforcement learning can recover task performance around a pretrained VLA under such physical failures without fault demonstrations, explicit fault identity, or a fault-free reference trajectory. We introduce Joint-Factorized Capability RL (JFCRL), which processes each actuator with the same shared temporal capability encoder, grounds each capability token using the current manipulator Jacobian, composes the resulting tokens with cross-actuator attention, and conditions a Soft Actor-Critic correction policy on the resulting capability latent. Self-supervised joint-motion, end-effector-motion, and kinematic-consistency objectives train the capability representation directly from execution, while the control policy is optimized from task reward. In a preliminary held-out-fault experiment, the base VLA succeeds in 20% of episodes, while JFCRL reaches 60% without additional adaptation to the held-out actuator fault. A global-context baseline fits the seen fault distribution strongly but does not show comparable held-out transfer. These results support actuator-factorized, kinematically grounded capability inference as a promising mechanism for VLA recovery under unseen embodiment faults.

Approach

Fault-aware execution through capability inference

REPAIR detects changes in physical capability from the discrepancy between commanded actions and the motion that the robot actually realizes.

Shared capability representation

A shared temporal encoder processes actuator response histories, while current kinematic information grounds those representations in their effect on robot motion.

Residual reinforcement learning

The inferred capability state conditions a residual reinforcement-learning controller that corrects the task-directed base action after a physical fault changes execution.

Generalization

The recovery mechanism is designed to transfer across actuator faults by learning from physical response and kinematic role rather than from a symbolic fault identity.

Results

In the current held-out-fault pilot, the base VLA succeeds in 20% of evaluation episodes, while JFCRL reaches 60% using the same shared recovery policy and without additional adaptation to the held-out actuator fault. The result suggests that the factorized capability representation transfers beyond the fault conditions used for training. A global-context wide-pool baseline performs strongly on its seen fault distribution but does not show comparable transfer to the held-out fault.
